Pattern 1
When Emma is silent → go easier
In all "no answer" rows (Q1–Q4), the robot has learned that known words and lower/same syntax are least bad. Pushing harder when she's disengaged consistently gave negative reward.
Pattern 2
When fully engaged → push both dimensions
In Q4 + "answered without prompt + long" (the best state), the best action is new word + higher syntax (+72). Emma handles challenge well when she's in the zone.
Pattern 3
New words re-engage better than familiar ones
Across most states, ∉κ columns slightly outperform ∈κ — novelty seems to spark Emma's curiosity even when her arousal is low. This is specific to her; another child's table could look different.
⚠ This is an illustrative example generated to demonstrate Q-table structure. The paper (Park et al. 2019) does not publish individual Q-tables — after 8 sessions, each child's table had ~423 episodes but the 120 state-action pairs were still sparse (each visited <4 times on average). A real table would be noisier and less converged than this one.
State space: 20 (5 task behaviors × 4 arousal quartiles) · Action space: 6 (2 lexical × 3 syntactic) · Total cells: 120
